California's Oceanfront Golf Courses — A Complete Guide to Playing Along the Pacific

← All Articles

There is something about oceanfront golf that landlocked courses can't replicate. The salt air, the changing wind, the way sunlight hits the water at different hours — it transforms a round of golf into something closer to a sensory experience. California, with over 840 miles of coastline, offers some of the most dramatic oceanfront layouts in the world. Here is what makes them worth the trip, and how to approach each one.

The Contenders

Pebble Beach Golf Links — Monterey Peninsula

The course that needs no introduction. Pebble Beach has anchored the California golf conversation for over a century, hosting six U.S. Opens and an annual PGA Tour event. Seven holes sit directly on the Pacific, and the stretch from the par-3 7th to the par-4 10th is arguably the most photographed sequence in golf. Green fees for non-resort guests run $625–$675, and tee times often book six to twelve months in advance. The resort-guest priority system means independent travelers should plan well ahead or work through an authorized provider.

Torrey Pines — San Diego

Two 18-hole courses perched on the cliffs above La Jolla. The South Course, a two-time U.S. Open host (2008, 2021), stretches beyond 7,700 yards from the tips — one of the longest layouts in major championship history. The North Course, redesigned by Tom Weiskopf in 2016, is shorter and more playable, with equally striking views. As San Diego municipal courses, green fees are notably accessible: $200–$300 for non-residents on the South Course, less on the North. The trade-off: tee times are released on a rolling basis and disappear quickly.

Trump National Los Angeles

Perched at the tip of the Palos Verdes Peninsula, this Pete Dye design features panoramic ocean views from all 18 holes — a rarity even among coastal courses. Waterfalls frame several holes, and the closing stretch plays along the bluff edge with the Pacific serving as an ever-present hazard. Green fees range from $180 to $320 depending on season.

Sandpiper Golf Club — Santa Barbara

A public-access course that delivers Pebble Beach-caliber oceanfront holes at a fraction of the cost. Several holes sit directly on the beach, with the back nine offering views of the Channel Islands. Green fees: $80–$150. Morning marine layer is common; afternoon tee times often provide clearer conditions.

What to Know Before Booking

  • Marine layer: Coastal fog is common in the morning, particularly May through July. After 10 AM tee times typically offer clearer skies.
  • Wind patterns: Afternoon sea breezes typically pick up around noon and can add one to two clubs of adjustment. Morning rounds are generally calmer.
  • Seasonal pricing: Rates are highest March–May and September–October. Summer offers longer daylight but can bring June Gloom. Winter is mild but brings shorter days and occasional rain.
  • Booking windows: The most in-demand courses (Pebble Beach, Torrey Pines South) require planning months ahead. Lesser-known options like Sandpiper and the Monterey Peninsula's other courses offer more flexibility.

The Bottom Line

California's oceanfront courses span a wide spectrum — from once-in-a-lifetime bucket-list venues to accessible public tracks that deliver coastal drama without coastal pricing. The key is understanding what each course demands in terms of budget, planning, and playability, then matching that to the kind of trip you want to have.
