Flying with Golf Clubs — The Complete Guide to Getting Your Gear There and Back

← All Articles

For golfers traveling internationally, the moment of truth comes not on the first tee but at the baggage carousel. Will your clubs arrive intact? The good news: airlines have standardized their policies around golf equipment, and with the right preparation, your clubs are statistically safer than most checked luggage. Here's how to maximize those odds.

Airline Policies at a Glance

All major transpacific carriers — Delta, United, American, Korean Air, Asiana, ANA, JAL — count a golf bag as one standard checked bag. Weight limits typically range from 23 kg (50 lbs) to 32 kg (70 lbs) depending on fare class. A full stand bag with 14 clubs, balls, shoes, and accessories usually weighs 18–20 kg — comfortably within standard limits. Oversize fees are rare for golf bags, as the 158 cm (L+W+H) limit accommodates most travel cases.

Key distinction: Some budget carriers treat golf bags as "special items" requiring additional fees. Always verify the specific policy for your ticket class before booking.

The Packing Protocol

  1. Use a hard case or premium soft case with internal support. Standard soft covers offer minimal protection. A hard case eliminates crush risk entirely. If using a soft case, invest in one with substantial internal padding and a rigid spine. Cost: $150–$400 for a quality travel case — significantly less than replacing a damaged driver.
  2. Remove driver and wood heads. Detach adjustable heads and store them separately in headcovers. The hosel is the most vulnerable point in transit.
  3. Weave a towel between irons. This prevents clubheads from rattling against each other during handling.
  4. Place a tracking device inside the bag. Bluetooth or GPS trackers add a layer of reassurance. If your bag gets misrouted — and it happens — you'll know exactly where it is.
  5. Pack sparingly within the bag. Every extra item adds weight and reduces internal cushioning. Shoes go in the outside pocket, not the main compartment.

At the Airport

Arrive at check-in with enough time to process oversize baggage (golf bags typically go through the oversize lane, which can be slower). Take a photo of your bag at the counter — it helps with claims if something goes wrong. Keep a change of clothes and essential toiletries in your carry-on; if your clubs are delayed, you can still function while they catch up.

At Your Destination

Most U.S. golf courses are self-service. No caddie, no bag drop attendant — you unload your own clubs from the rental car and return them after the round. Rental clubs are available at nearly every course ($40–$80 per round) and are a viable backup option. If you're playing only one or two rounds during a multi-purpose trip, renting may be more practical than hauling clubs across the Pacific.
