Beyond Golf — How to Build a Multi-Sport California Trip

← All Articles

California is one of the few places on earth where a traveler can play 18 holes in the morning, watch a major league baseball game in the evening, and wake up the next day to hike in a national park — all within a single metropolitan area. For golfers traveling with non-golfers, or for anyone who wants a trip that's about more than golf, the state's density of professional sports, outdoor recreation, and cultural attractions creates opportunities that are worth building an itinerary around.

Golf + Baseball: The Los Angeles/San Diego Axis

The Dodgers (Los Angeles) and Padres (San Diego) play in stadiums that are within 30 minutes of major golf courses for six months of the year (April–September). A day structured around a morning tee time and an evening first pitch is logistically clean: the rhythms of baseball and golf complement each other naturally. Dodger Stadium's field-level seats behind home plate or along the first-base line offer proximity to players that television can't replicate. Petco Park in San Diego is widely considered one of the best ballparks in America, with a location in the Gaslamp Quarter that makes post-game dining a short walk rather than a parking-lot ordeal.

Golf + Basketball/Hockey: Winter Options

The NBA and NHL seasons (October–April) align with peak golf season in the desert and shoulder season on the coast. Los Angeles alone fields two NBA teams (Lakers, Clippers) and one NHL team (Kings), all playing at Crypto.com Arena in downtown LA. The arena is a 45-minute drive from several inland courses and within 90 minutes of coastal golf. The combination of morning golf and evening indoor sports is particularly effective in winter, when shorter days make afternoon golf less practical.

Golf + National Parks

Several of America's most visited national parks sit within driving distance of major golf destinations: Joshua Tree (45 minutes from Palm Springs), Zion (45 minutes from Mesquite), Yosemite (2.5 hours from the Bay Area), Sequoia and Kings Canyon (2 hours from Central Valley courses). The practical move: structure the trip with golf at the beginning and end, and a 2–3 day park visit in the middle. This format works for mixed-interest groups where not everyone plays, and it creates a trip that feels genuinely varied rather than single-focus.

Practical Planning

  • Check game schedules before booking flights. MLB schedules are released in August for the following season. NBA and NHL schedules come out in August–September. Aligning your travel dates with a home stand is the single most important planning step.
  • Weekday games are easier and cheaper. Weekend Dodgers games sell out. Tuesday–Thursday games offer better seat availability and often lower prices.
  • Stadium tours operate on non-game days. If the schedule doesn't align, a stadium tour (Dodger Stadium, Petco Park) provides behind-the-scenes access — dugouts, press boxes, trophy rooms — that isn't available on game days.
