California Golf Through the Seasons — A Month-by-Month Planning Guide

← All Articles

California's golf map changes shape with the calendar. The same course that delivers a pristine, 72-degree round in March might offer a fog-bound, wind-whipped test in June — or a bargain-rate, wide-open tee sheet in September. Here's how the state plays, month by month, and what that means for trip planning.

Winter (December–February)

Coast: Southern California coastal courses (LA, Orange County, San Diego) operate at full capacity. Daytime temperatures hold at 60–68°F (15–20°C), ideal for walking. Rain is possible but typically passes in a day. This is when coastal courses are at their best — green, lush, and comfortable.

Desert: Palm Springs and the Coachella Valley are in peak season. Green fees are at their annual maximum ($200–$500 at premium courses). Tee sheets fill weeks in advance. If you want desert golf, book early — but also budget accordingly.

Northern California: Monterey and the Bay Area are cool (50–60°F) and can be wet. Pebble Beach operates year-round but winter brings shorter days and occasional closures for weather. Green fees dip slightly from summer peaks.

Spring (March–May)

The sweet spot. March and April are arguably the best all-around months for California golf. Temperatures are mild everywhere — desert courses are warm but not yet hot, coastal courses are clear and bright. Wildflowers appear in inland valleys. Green fees begin transitioning from winter peaks to summer rates. May brings "May Gray" to coastal areas — morning overcast that typically burns off by noon — and the start of summer pricing at many resorts.

Summer (June–September)

"June Gloom" is real. Coastal areas from Santa Barbara to San Diego experience morning marine layer that can delay visibility until 10:00–11:00 AM. Afternoons, however, are reliably clear and warm. This is peak season for Northern California coastal golf (Monterey, Bodega Bay, Santa Cruz), where temperatures rarely exceed 75°F.

The desert shift. Palm Springs, Coachella Valley, and Las Vegas enter their extreme season. Dawn tee times are mandatory. Green fees plummet. The courses remain in good condition — most overseed for year-round play — and the economics are compelling for early risers.

Fall (October–November)

The best-kept secret. October in Southern California is nearly perfect: low humidity, crystal skies, temperatures steady at 68–78°F. Green fees have dropped from summer peaks but conditions remain excellent. Courses are less crowded as families return to school-year routines. November brings cooler temperatures and the start of overseeding at some desert and inland courses — always check individual course schedules before booking. Fall is also the prime season for the Central Coast (Paso Robles, Santa Barbara, Monterey), where harvest-season energy adds a non-golf dimension to the trip.
