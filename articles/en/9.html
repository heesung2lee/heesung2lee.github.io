Building a Trip Around a Tournament — How to Combine Spectator Golf with Playing Golf

← All Articles

Watching professional golf in person and playing golf are two different hobbies. But a well-structured trip can accommodate both — and the result is often a more memorable experience than either activity alone. The key is understanding how tournament weeks affect the surrounding golf ecosystem, and planning accordingly.

The Tournament Effect on Local Courses

When a PGA Tour event comes to town, nearby courses don't shut down — but they do get busier. Practice rounds (Monday–Wednesday) draw crowds of golf-obsessed visitors who want to play in the mornings before heading to the course to watch. Thursday–Sunday, the tournament occupies the attention, and local courses see a slight dip in demand — which can create opportunities for visitors who plan around the broadcast schedule. The best local tee times during tournament week are often Thursday and Friday mornings.

Structuring the Trip

  • Monday–Wednesday: Play golf in the mornings. Attend practice rounds in the afternoons. Practice rounds are less crowded, players are more relaxed, and access to the course is generally more open. Many tournaments allow phones and cameras during practice rounds but not during competition — this is the window for photos and up-close observation.
  • Thursday–Friday: Play early (6:30–7:30 AM tee times). Finish by 11:00 AM. Head to the tournament for the afternoon wave of players. These are the best days for combining playing and watching — weekend crowds haven't arrived, and energy builds through the afternoon.
  • Saturday–Sunday: Choose one: play or watch. Trying to do both on a weekend tournament day usually means compromising both. If watching is the priority, book a late-afternoon round on Sunday after the trophy presentation, when courses are empty and the tournament energy carries into an unhurried twilight round.

VIP and Hospitality Options

Most PGA Tour events offer tiered hospitality packages beyond general admission. These range from shared hospitality tents with food and beverage to private suites on signature holes with player access. Entry-level hospitality ($200–$500/day) typically includes shaded seating, upgraded food, and dedicated restrooms — a significant quality-of-life improvement for a long day outdoors. Premium packages ($1,500–$5,000+) add open bars, hosted meals, and sometimes post-round player Q&A sessions. For a milestone trip — a major birthday, a career achievement — the premium tier transforms the experience from "attending a tournament" to something closer to "being hosted at one."

Which Tournaments Work Best

The West Coast Swing (January–February: Palm Springs, Torrey Pines, Pebble Beach, Phoenix, Riviera) offers the most efficient golf-plus-spectator combinations. The weather is mild, the courses are accessible, and the tournament venues themselves are bucket-list courses that visitors can play outside of tournament weeks. The FedEx Cup playoffs (August) concentrate elite fields but also coincide with peak summer heat in many regions. The U.S. Open (June) moves locations annually but always delivers the most intense on-course atmosphere of any golf event — worth building a trip around if the venue aligns with your travel window.

Practical Notes

  • Book lodging early — tournament weeks fill hotels within a 30-mile radius, often months in advance.
  • Rental cars during tournament weeks cost more and sell out faster. Reserve as soon as dates are confirmed.
  • Practice rounds are significantly cheaper than competition days and often provide better player access.
  • Many tournaments offer discounted tickets for weekend afternoons — the leaders tee off latest, and arriving at 2:00 PM on Sunday still provides several hours of meaningful golf.
